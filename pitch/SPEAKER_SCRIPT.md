# Trace · KIT Gründerschmiede · 10-minute speaker script

Tone: calm, precise, a scientist who happens to have taste. Never oversell. Let the honesty benchmark and the live demo do the bragging. Pauses are marked **[beat]**. Total ≈ 10 min with room for one interruption.

Advance the deck on the cues marked ▸. Each ▸ is one keypress (it plays the next animation step, then the next slide).

---

## Slide 1 · Title (≈ 40s)

*(Open on the gray chain PROMPT → CONTEXT → ANSWER. Let it sit for three seconds before you speak.)*

"Every AI assistant on the market works left to right. You prompt it, it gathers context, it answers." **[beat]**

▸ *(the morph fires: CONTEXT arcs to the front, ignites orange)*

"Trace runs the same three words in the opposite order. Context first, then prompt, then answer. **[beat]** That inversion is the whole company, because it means the assistant never has to record anything to be useful."

▸ *(TRACE wordmark and tagline appear)*

"We're Trace. Perfect recall, zero recordings. I'll show you a working prototype at the end, on my phone."

## Slide 2 · The problem (≈ 70s)

▸

"Today you get two kinds of AI memory, and both are bad deals."

▸ *(the 'forget' vignette types in and blurs)*

"On one side, the assistants that respect your privacy are blind to your physical life. They live inside your apps. They cannot help you with the name you just forgot at a conference, because your real context is the room, the label, the person in front of you, and they never see it."

▸ *(the news clippings stack in; the storage counter climbs to 50)*

"On the other side are the devices that do see your world. They see it by recording it. Seven million camera glasses sold last year. And the two products built purely to record everything, Humane's Pin and the Rewind pendant, are already dead or absorbed. A single wearer generates about fifty gigabytes of other people's faces and voices a day, sitting on a server, searchable forever."

▸ *(the hinge line draws)*

"Both sides lose, and the reason is architectural. Any assistant that has to store your context in order to use it ends up here. So we changed the architecture."

---

## Slide 3 · The solution (≈ 70s)

▸

"Our principle is three words: perceive, don't record."

▸ *(the pipeline lights up node by node, then the frame shatters)*

"A frame comes off the camera. On the device, local AI reads it and writes one line of typed evidence. 'Desk, black keyboard, the label reads DEWALT, 14:32.' Then, and this is the important part, the raw frame is destroyed. Milliseconds. What survives is a line of text, encrypted, on your own device, that you can ask about any time."

▸ *(the two pillars appear)*

"That gives Trace two properties that are true by architecture, not by promise. **[beat]** One: it never stores what it sees, so there is nothing to leak, subpoena, or breach. Surveillance is not against our policy, it is structurally impossible. Two:"

▸ *(the refusal demo types itself)*

"it never pretends to know. Every answer cites the evidence it was built from, or it refuses. Ask it how many unicorns are on the desk and it tells you it has never seen one."

## Slide 4 · Status (≈ 55s)

▸

"This is not a concept. It runs, it cites, and it refuses, today."

▸ *(the three panels build; evidence rows cascade; the refusal lands)*

"Live camera perception to cited answers, entirely on local models. No cloud, no API keys, no frames kept. On the left, the perception overlay as it sees. In the middle, a real answer with its evidence rows and a calibrated confidence badge. On the right, the refusal."

▸

"And here is the number I care about most. On a reproducible benchmark battery, one command anyone can run, Trace scores zero percent confident-wrong answers. It would rather say 'I don't know' than make something up. I have not met another AI assistant that can put that number on a slide."

## Slide 8 · Competitors (≈ 55s)

▸

"So where does everyone sit."

▸ *(axes draw; competitor dots drop into three quadrants; one stays empty)*

"Two axes: how much physical-world context you capture, and how little you retain. The privacy-disciplined assistants are top-left, blind to the world. The wearables and the recorders are along the bottom, seeing the world by keeping it. **[beat]** The top-right, physical context with zero retention, has always been empty, because physical context has always required retention. That's why the pure recorders keep dying."

▸ *(Trace ignites in the empty quadrant)*

"Trace's architecture is the first way into that quadrant."

▸ *(the pre-empt box appears)*

"And the question you're forming: why won't Apple or Google just build this. Because their business models monetize retained data, and their privacy stack deliberately stops at your digital life. A zero-retention physical layer contradicts their economics. If the giants want this, they license it or they buy it, and both of those require Trace to exist first."

## Slide 9 · Business model (≈ 60s)

▸

"The model has two streams. The device wins adoption; the layer earns the money."

▸ *(stream one and the charm grid build)*

"Stream one is consumer, and it's the adoption engine, not the profit engine. A low membership, four to six euros, with the charm included. It's display-less, so we strip out the single most expensive part of a smart glass, more than half the bill of materials. Consumer margin is about zero, by design. We can afford that forever, because the AI runs on the customer's own phone, so our marginal compute cost is essentially zero. A cloud-AI competitor structurally cannot match that price."

▸ *(stream two and the device tags build)*

"Stream two is the profit engine: licensing that context layer, per device, to eyewear brands, wearable makers, and assistant platforms. Software margins. Every membership user is proof and leverage for those deals."

▸ *(the precedent line appears)*

"The precedent is real. Arm doesn't sell chips and Dolby doesn't sell speakers; each earns over a billion a year licensing a layer into devices they don't build. Trace doesn't need to profit on charms. It needs to become the layer every device licenses."

# Q&A prep — the three questions you will get

**1. "Why won't Apple or Google just build this and crush you?"**
Short answer: incentives, not capability. Their revenue and their engagement metrics depend on retained data; a truly zero-retention layer is orthogonal to how they make money, and it competes with their own capture ambitions. Historically, when a hard-to-build, incentive-misaligned capability appears, incumbents license or acquire it rather than rebuild it against their own grain. Either outcome is a good outcome for us, and both require us to exist and to own the benchmark and the patents first. Add: our edge compounds in Europe, where retention is a growing liability for them and a non-issue for us.

**2. "You're a solo, non-CS founder. Why should we back that?"**
Don't apologize; reframe. The honesty benchmark is the answer. I built every layer of a working system alone, on consumer hardware, and the thing that a measurement scientist brought to it, calibrated refusal, is the actual moat. That said, I know EXIST favors teams, and I agree. Completing the team with a product and ML co-founder is not a someday item, it's an explicit horizon-two milestone, and it's the first thing I want your network's help with. I mentor one researcher already, so I know how to bring someone in and grow them.

**3. "If consumer margin is zero, how is this a business?"**
The consumer device is customer acquisition and proof, priced to spread, exactly like a razor sold near cost. The margin lives in licensing the context layer, which is software at near-100% gross margin, benchmarked against Arm and Dolby, both billion-a-year licensing businesses that don't manufacture the devices they're in. Every low-priced membership user makes the licensing pitch stronger, because it's more proof the layer works and more leverage in the negotiation.

**4. (Likely) "How do you actually measure zero-percent-wrong?"**
A fixed battery of questions with known answers, including questions whose honest answer is 'I don't know,' run against the real local model with one command. We count three things: correct-on-present, refuse-on-absent, and the one that matters, confident-and-wrong. The build fails, literally exits red, if a single confident-wrong answer appears. It's reproducible; I can run it for you.

**5. (Likely) "Frames on the user's phone, isn't that still capture?"**
The raw frame exists only in memory, on the user's own device, for the milliseconds it takes to turn it into a line of text, then it's gone. Nothing is written to disk, nothing leaves the phone, nothing reaches us. There is no footage to hand over, because there is no footage. That's the difference between processing and recording.
