FOUNDER OPERATIONS · RESEARCHED 21 JULY 2026

Founding TRACE in Germany

What the EXIST programme actually is, how this company gets funded, and what German residence law says about building a startup while holding a student visa. Every factual claim below carries its source. The parts that are uncertain are marked uncertain.

Read this first

This is not legal advice, and it is not immigration advice. It is a researched briefing assembled from primary sources so that the right questions can be asked of the right offices. German residence law is applied by individual case officers at the local Ausländerbehörde with substantial discretion; two applicants with identical facts can receive different answers. Nothing here should be relied on without confirmation from (a) the Ausländerbehörde Karlsruhe, (b) KIT Gründerschmiede, and (c) for anything consequential, an immigration lawyer (Fachanwalt für Migrationsrecht).

Sources age. Two of the official BMWK answers cited below are from 2016 and 2020, and the ministry states its info pool is no longer updated. One widely-cited figure on this topic is already outdated — see the 140-day finding in §4.2, which contradicts the government's own page.

Legend used throughout: VERIFIED confirmed against a primary or official source today · DATED official but from an outdated page · MUST CONFIRM not resolvable from public sources.

1 · What EXIST is

EXIST is the German federal government's programme for turning university and research-institute work into companies. It is funded by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy (BMWE) together with the European Social Fund (ESF Plus). It is not a loan and not an equity investment: the startup grant is a stipend, and the state takes no shares. VERIFIED

There are three lines that matter to TRACE.

1.1 · EXIST Startup Grant (EXIST-Gründungsstipendium)

The main instrument: it pays founders a monthly stipend for twelve months to take a science-based idea from concept to company. Amounts are per person, per month, by qualification:

QualificationMonthly stipend
Doctorate (PhD)€3,000
University graduate€2,500
Vocational qualification€2,000
Student (≥ half of degree completed)€1,000
Per dependent child+ €150

Plus, for the team: up to €30,000 for materials and equipment (up to €10,000 for single-founder ventures) and €5,000 for coaching. Duration is 12 months. VERIFIED

The €18,000 sentence

Student rate is €1,000/month. Graduate rate is €2,500/month. Over the twelve-month grant that difference is €18,000 — and the graduate window stays open for five years after leaving the university. Applying as a student rather than as a graduate is, in cash terms, the single most expensive scheduling decision available in this programme. §6 returns to what this means for sequencing.

Eligibility. Students who have completed at least half their degree; graduates and former research staff up to five years after leaving; researchers at universities and research institutions. Teams of at most three people. At least one team member must hold a university degree, and no more than one may hold a vocational qualification or a degree obtained more than five years ago. VERIFIED

How you apply. This is the detail most people get wrong: applications are submitted through the university, not by the individual. The university's startup office is the applicant-facing channel and the sponsor of the application. For TRACE that office is KIT Gründerschmiede, which runs an explicit grant application support service and is one of the largest university startup centres in Germany. VERIFIED

Deadlines and paperwork. Rolling submission through to the end of 2027 under the current ESF Plus funding period — there is no annual cliff, but there is a programme horizon. The full deadline picture, the eight required attachments, and the prescribed structure of the concept paper are set out in §2. VERIFIED

Timeline. Official review time after submission is three months. Universities that run these applications routinely advise budgeting three to six months of part-time work on the application itself, i.e. roughly six to nine months from starting the application to money arriving. VERIFIED (TUM startup office guidance; treat the three-month official figure as firm and the rest as planning experience.)

1.2 · EXIST Research Transfer (EXIST-Forschungstransfer)

The larger, deep-tech instrument, for development-intensive projects that need real engineering before a company is viable. Two phases:

  • Phase I — up to €250,000 in material expenses (equipment, supplies, IP rights, coaching), plus personnel costs for three research positions and one business position.
  • Phase II — up to €180,000 toward operational expenses after company formation.

VERIFIED

Which line fits TRACE

Worth taking seriously rather than defaulting to the Startup Grant. TRACE's honest state — thinking machinery implemented, hardware senses still on fakes, a falsification gate that needs real captured weeks — is development-intensive work before commercial viability, which is precisely what Research Transfer exists for. Phase I funding four salaried positions would also solve the bus-factor-one problem the thesis names as a risk, and it does so without diluting equity.

The counter-argument is speed and fit: the Startup Grant is lighter, faster, and better matched to a solo founder with a co-founder seat still open. This is a real strategic fork and KIT Gründerschmiede is the right party to resolve it — they will have seen both routes run from KIT many times. MUST CONFIRM

1.3 · EXIST Women

A ten-month track for prospective female founders: €2,000 in-kind support plus up to €2,000/month for a maximum of three months, with workshops and mentoring. KIT runs its own EXIST-Women line. Not applicable to the current founder, but directly relevant when the team grows. VERIFIED

2 · Deadlines and paperwork

The most common question from anyone weighing whether to join, and the one where vague answers waste months. The short version: there is no application deadline, but there are hard deadlines once you are funded — and the application itself cannot be assembled alone, because four of the eight required documents must be signed by other people.

2.1 · Deadlines: the honest shape

Deadline typeReality
Application deadline None. Rolling submission through to the end of 2027 (current ESF Plus funding period). You submit when the package is ready. VERIFIED
Programme horizon End of 2027 is a real cliff. Whatever follows is not yet announced.
University-internal Set by the startup network, not by EXIST. Some universities run monthly submission windows. KIT's own cadence must be asked for directly. MUST CONFIRM
Official review Three months from submission to decision. VERIFIED
Realistic total Six to nine months from starting the application to money arriving — three to six of those spent writing it part-time. Plan from this number, not the three-month one.
The deadlines that actually bite are inside the grant

Signing Anlage 3 commits the founder to a fixed milestone calendar over the twelve funded months. These are contractual, not advisory:

  • ~Month 1 — kickoff meeting (online) with the university supervisors and the project agency (Projektträger Jülich).
  • Early — select a coach together with the startup network, and agree a written coaching/supervision roadmap; attend a "Gründungsteam" seminar.
  • Throughout — at least two presentation dates on business-plan progress before the startup network and coach.
  • Month 5 — interim business-plan status discussed with the network (business model, customer benefit, unique selling points, market and competition), then the completed "Bewertung Zwischenstand" form and the interim presentation sent to the project agency.
  • End of month 10 — the final business plan must be with the startup network early enough for them to forward it, with their evaluation, a results presentation and the project questionnaire, to the project agency.
  • Ongoing — notify the project agency of material changes to the work plan.

Read that calendar next to the twelve-month clock and the real constraint appears: the business plan is due at month ten, not month twelve. The last two months are not build time — they are the runway onto whatever comes next, which is why §3.3's point about starting the co-investor conversation early is a scheduling fact rather than advice.

2.3 · What the concept paper must contain

The Ideenpapier is where the application is won or lost, and EXIST prescribes its structure and even its page budget. Fifteen pages, allocated as follows:

SectionBudgetWhat it must answer
1. Business idea~6 pp
1.1 Innovation~3 pp The technology / product innovation or knowledge-based service, and the strategy for protecting it (IP rights or explicit alternative strategy)
1.2 Customer benefit~0.5 ppTarget group and potential customers; what problem the idea solves
1.3 Origin story~0.5 pp Who originated the idea, links to prior projects, and the connection to the applying university — studies, department, preparatory work
1.4 State of development~1 p What exists now, what will be built during the stipend, and what is additionally required for a first market-ready version
1.5 Project plan~1 pWork plan for the funding period, and the outlook to market entry or follow-on financing
2. Team~3 pp Qualifications and relevant experience including business knowledge; planned division of roles and shareholding structure (with anticipated split); advisors, mentors, industry contacts; an explicit statement of what competences or resources the team lacks and how the gaps will be closed; and a section on the contribution to women in founding and wider team diversity
3. Commercial implementation~5 pp
3.1 Competition~2 ppKey competitors and differentiation — unique selling points, tabular comparison acceptable
3.2 Market entry~1 p Entry segment; implementation partnerships; which customers have already been spoken to and what the feedback was (attach LOIs); required approvals, certifications, industry standards
3.3 Financial planning~1.5 pp Pricing and its derivation versus competitors; a simple P&L with sales planning at least to break-even; financing concept
3.4 Sustainability~0.5 ppQualitative and quantitative contribution to the federal government's sustainability goals (SDGs)
4. Budget planning~0.5 pp Rough tabular breakdown of the €5,000 coaching budget and the €30,000 materials budget (€10,000 for single founders). Explicitly adjustable during the funding period.

Confidentiality is stated on the form itself: all information in the concept paper is treated confidentially and not passed to third parties. VERIFIED

Where TRACE is already strong, and where it is exposed

Strong. Sections 1.1, 1.3, 1.4 and 1.5 are effectively already written — the thesis covers innovation, origin, honest state of development, and roadmap in more depth than fifteen pages can hold, and §1.4's demand to separate "what exists now" from "what will be built" maps exactly onto the thesis's what is real today ledger. Section 3.4 on sustainability has a real answer rather than a manufactured one: an architecture that never stores video is a dramatically smaller compute and storage footprint than the storage-first alternative.

Exposed. Three places. Section 2 requires business knowledge on the team and an explicit statement of what is missing — with a single technical founder and an open co-founder seat, that gap must be named, not hidden (the guidance elsewhere is that three technical founders can apply if one commits to the business role and can evidence it). Section 3.2 asks which customers have actually been spoken to — TRACE currently has architecture and measurements, not customer conversations, and that is a gap to start closing now, because LOIs cannot be produced retroactively. Section 3.3 demands a P&L to break-even, which does not yet exist in any document.

2.4 · What this means for a prospective cofounder

Three consequences worth being explicit about, because they affect whether joining is attractive:

  • You cannot be added silently later. Each founder files their own Anlage 1, 3 and 7 with their own certificates — and, for non-EU nationals, their own residence documentation. Joining before submission is straightforward; joining after approval is a change to the funded project and must be negotiated with the project agency.
  • The stipend is per person. Two funded founders at graduate rate is €5,000/month between them, on top of the shared €30,000 materials budget — the grant scales with the team up to three.
  • Shareholding is declared up front. Section 2 of the concept paper requires the anticipated equity split. That conversation happens before submission, in writing, not afterwards.

3 · The money, honestly

3.1 · What the stipend is not

Three facts that people consistently discover too late:

The stipend is taxable

The EXIST founder stipend is not tax-free. It must be declared on the income tax return (Anlage SO). German tax guidance puts the practical burden at roughly 15–30% depending on individual circumstances. Business expenses and insurance contributions are deductible against it. Budget the net, not the headline. VERIFIED (tax-advisory sources; the exact rate is individual — confirm with a Steuerberater).

You insure yourself

Recipients are responsible for their own health, care and pension insurance, as self-employed people are. The BMWK confirms the stipend counts as income for the purposes of calculating health-insurance contributions. Critically: student health insurance rates are tied to student status — losing that status changes the monthly cost materially, and that delta has to be modelled before accepting a stipend. DATED (BMWK answer, August 2020; ministry states the pool is no longer updated) MUST CONFIRM with the intended Krankenkasse.

It demands full time

The grant requires founders to dedicate themselves full-time to the venture. Side activity is capped at roughly five hours per week (20 hours per month). This is not a formality — it is the constraint that collides with student status, and §6 is about that collision. VERIFIED (German founder-guidance sources; confirm the exact wording in the current funding guidelines with PtJ / KIT).

3.2 · Company formation timing

The company must not already be founded when the grant period starts. Incorporating during the funding period is permitted, but must be strictly separated from the funded project at the university. Founding too early disqualifies; founding at the right moment is a coordination question to run through the startup consultant before incorporating, particularly where IP is to be held by the company rather than personally. VERIFIED

Whether the venture may generate revenue during the funding period is not resolvable from public sources — guidance is inconsistent, and one IHK source suggests revenue generation is not permitted under the corresponding residence status. This matters if TRACE were to take early licensing income. MUST CONFIRM — ask Projektträger Jülich directly at ptj-exist-gruendungsstipendium@fz-juelich.de.

3.3 · The ladder after EXIST

EXIST is twelve months. The question every EXIST founder faces in month seven is what comes next. Two rungs are relevant to a company seated in Baden-Württemberg:

InstrumentShapeNotes
Start-up BW Pre-Seed
Ministry of Economic Affairs BW + L-Bank		 Typically €160,000 public, matched by a co-investor up to €160,000; in individual cases total funding up to €640,000 Requires company seat and investment location in Baden-Württemberg — KIT/Karlsruhe qualifies. Runs in selection rounds; rounds were actively awarding in 2026. VERIFIED
EXIST Research Transfer Phase II Up to €180,000 operational Only if the venture entered via Research Transfer rather than the Startup Grant — another reason §1.2's fork matters early.

The strategic point: Start-up BW Pre-Seed requires a co-investor. That means the twelve EXIST months are not only build time, they are the window in which an investor relationship has to be started. Working backwards from a co-investor commitment is the actual EXIST timeline, not the grant's own end date.

4 · The visa: what the law actually says

This section quotes the statute rather than summarising commentary, because the difference between the German modal verbs kann (may) and soll (shall) is worth a great deal of money and several months here.

4.1 · The general founder permit — § 21(1) AufenthG

The default route to a self-employment residence permit requires three things simultaneously:

  • an economic interest or a regional need exists,
  • the activity is expected to have positive effects on the economy, and
  • financing is secured through equity or a credit commitment.

The verb is kann — "may be granted." Discretionary. In practice this route demands a full business plan: company profile, market analysis, three-year revenue forecast, liquidity plan, financing plan, and evidence of the founder's qualifications. VERIFIED against the statute.

4.2 · What a student permit allows — § 16b AufenthG

A student residence permit permits employment of up to 140 working days per year (Arbeitstagekonto), quoted from the current statute:

„Beschäftigungen, die insgesamt bis zu 140 Arbeitstage im Jahr nicht überschreiten dürfen."

A live contradiction — worth knowing before you quote a number at anyone

The federal government's own founder portal still states the limit as "120 volle oder 240 halbe Tage" — the pre-reform rule. The statute currently in force says 140 working days. The 120/240 figure is widely repeated across advice sites and is outdated. If a number is going to be cited in a conversation with an authority, cite § 16b(3) itself, not a portal summary. VERIFIED against gesetze-im-internet.de · DATED on the BMWK page.

Note also what § 16b does not contain: any mention of self-employment. The student permit governs dependent employment. Self-employment is a separate question, answered by the next provision.

4.3 · The side door — § 21(6) AufenthG

This is the provision that makes founding-while-studying legally possible at all:

"A foreigner who holds or is granted a residence permit for another purpose may be permitted to engage in self-employment while retaining that purpose of residence, if the permissions required under other provisions have been granted or promised."

Three consequences, all confirmed by the BMWK's own guidance:

  • The Ausländerbehörde must approve the self-employment separately — the student permit does not carry it automatically.
  • The activity must not endanger the success of the studies in its scope.
  • Explicitly: "whoever wants to start a full-time operation will not receive the permission."

VERIFIED (§ 21(6) statute + BMWK founder portal). That last clause is the crux of §6.

4.4 · The graduate's advantage — § 21(2a) AufenthG

The most important paragraph in this document for TRACE's founder:

"A foreigner who has successfully completed a course of study at a state or state-recognised higher education institution … in the federal territory, or who holds a residence permit as a researcher or scientist under §§ 18b, 18d, 19c(1) or an EU Blue Card, shall be granted a residence permit for the purpose of self-employment, notwithstanding subsection (1)."

Why this changes everything

Two words carry the weight. Soll — "shall," not "may": the authority's discretion narrows sharply, and the permit is the expected outcome rather than a favour. Abweichend von Absatz 1 — "notwithstanding subsection (1)": the three hard tests of §4.1 (economic interest, positive economic effects, secured financing) do not have to be met.

A graduate of KIT is squarely inside this provision. So is a researcher holding a research permit. This is the intended door, and it is a much wider door than §21(1). VERIFIED against the statute.

The one condition — and a specific risk for this founder

§21(2a) requires a connection between the self-employment and the knowledge acquired during the studies or research. This is not a formality, and it deserves candour here: TRACE's founder is a materials scientist whose thesis is at the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics, while TRACE is an on-device AI and systems company. A case officer could reasonably ask how the two connect.

The answer exists and is strong — measurement science, instrumentation, calibration discipline, and evaluation methodology run straight through TRACE's architecture, and the thesis makes that lineage explicit — but it is an argument that must be prepared in writing before the appointment, not improvised at the counter. Treat it as the single highest-risk item in the residence application. MUST CONFIRM how strictly Karlsruhe's ABH reads the connection requirement.

4.5 · The bridge — § 20 AufenthG

After successfully completing studies in Germany under §16b, a graduate receives a residence permit of up to 18 months to seek employment or take up qualified activity; employment during that period is governed by §§ 18a, 18b, 18d, 18g, 19c and § 21 — that final cross-reference means self-employment is explicitly within scope of the post-study period. VERIFIED

Eighteen months is, not coincidentally, longer than the twelve months of an EXIST grant. That overlap is the safest structural window available.

4.6 · Duration and permanence — § 21(4)

A self-employment permit is issued for at most three years. After three years a settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis, i.e. permanent residence) may be granted if the person has been self-employed for those three years, the business shows prospects of sustainable further development, and income adequately secures the founder's and dependents' living costs. VERIFIED

Two footnotes: applicants over 45 must additionally show adequate pension provision (§21(3)) — not currently applicable. And §21(5) provides a separate, lighter route for freiberuflich (liberal profession) activity, which is generally not the right classification for a product company but is worth knowing exists.

Indicative official fees: around €100 for the initial permit and €93 for renewal, plus the real costs — business plan preparation, certified translations, and legal advice. MUST CONFIRM current fees with the ABH; these figures come from law-firm summaries, not a fee schedule read today.

5 · Non-EU nationals and EXIST

The reassuring headline, from the BMWK directly: citizenship is not a criterion.

"Citizenship is not important when applying for an EXIST Business Start-up Grant — even being part of an international team. Non-EU citizens have the same chance of receiving funding, if they have finished a university degree and own a residence permit for at least one year in Germany." DATED — BMWK info pool, February 2016.

The operative constraint is not nationality but permit coverage: a non-EU recipient needs a residence and work permit valid for the entire grant period, and at application time at least a preliminary confirmation from the Ausländerbehörde. The parallel state programme in North Rhine-Westphalia states the requirement more precisely, and it is a good proxy for what will be asked: a residence permit for the purpose of gainful employment (Aufenthaltserlaubnis zu Erwerbszwecken) or permanent residence, valid for the whole funding period, with no supplementary-sheet restriction incompatible with working full-time on the startup. VERIFIED

The sequencing trap this creates

Read the two requirements together and a chicken-and-egg problem appears. EXIST wants a permit covering the full twelve months before it grants. The ABH wants to see secured funding and a credible venture before it grants a permit. The standard resolution is the preliminary confirmation (Vorabzustimmung / provisional certificate) from the ABH, which EXIST accepts at application time — which is exactly why the ABH conversation must happen early and in parallel with the EXIST application, never after it.

6 · The collision, and how to sequence around it

Put the findings side by side and one contradiction dominates everything else:

EXIST requiresStudent status permits
Full-time dedication to the venture; side activity capped at ~5 hrs/week Self-employment only if it does not endanger the studies — and explicitly, full-time operations will not be approved under §21(6)
€1,000/month at student rate €2,500/month at graduate rate — a €18,000 swing over the grant

These two cannot both be satisfied at once. A student cannot simultaneously be a full-time founder under EXIST's rules and a student whose studies are not endangered under §21(6)'s rules. This is a structural conflict, not a paperwork problem, and no amount of application-writing dissolves it.

The sequence that resolves it
  1. Finish the degree. It converts the €1,000 student rate into the €2,500 graduate rate, removes the §21(6) "must not endanger studies" constraint entirely, and unlocks §21(2a)'s shall-grant. The graduate window then stays open for five years.
  2. Prepare the EXIST application during the final study phase — legitimately, within the 140-day employment allowance and the ~5 hrs/week side-activity ceiling. Application preparation is not full-time operation. Budget six to nine months.
  3. Open the ABH conversation in parallel, not after. Target the preliminary confirmation that EXIST accepts, and go in with the §21(2a) connection argument (§4.4) written down.
  4. On graduation, land on §20 — the 18-month post-study permit, which explicitly includes §21 self-employment and comfortably covers a 12-month grant.
  5. Convert to §21(2a) as the venture becomes the primary activity; §21(4) then puts permanent residence three years out.

One honest caveat on that sequence: it is assembled from the statutes and official guidance cited here, and it is internally consistent — but it has not been validated by anyone with authority over the decision. It is a hypothesis to be tested against the ABH and KIT Gründerschmiede, in exactly the spirit the rest of this project applies to its own claims.
